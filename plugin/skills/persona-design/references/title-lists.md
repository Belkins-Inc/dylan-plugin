# Title lists: worked examples

Examples of the shape, not lists to copy. Build each from the company's brief.

## Support leader (consumer e-commerce, 50 to 1,000 employees)

**Titles:** Head of Customer Support, Head of Customer Service, Head of Customer Experience, Head of
CX, Director of Customer Support, Customer Service Director, Customer Experience Director, VP
Customer Support, VP Customer Experience, VP of Customer Service, Chief Customer Officer, Customer
Support Lead, Customer Service Manager, Customer Care Manager, Head of Customer Care

**Description:** Senior people who own the customer support function (the team, its tools and its
response times) in a consumer-facing company. In companies under about 200 employees this is often
a Customer Service Manager or Support Lead reporting to a founder; above that, a Head, Director or
VP. Excludes agents, specialists, coordinators, shift team leads, and Customer Success roles that
manage business accounts rather than support.

## Finance decision maker (software, 20 to 500 employees)

**Titles:** CFO, Chief Financial Officer, VP Finance, Head of Finance, Finance Director, Director of
Finance, Financial Controller, Controller, Head of FP&A

**Description:** The person who signs off on finance tooling and owns the finance team's processes.
Under about 50 employees there is often no CFO and the Head of Finance or a Controller decides;
above about 300 the CFO delegates tooling to a VP Finance or Controller. Excludes accountants,
analysts, bookkeepers, payroll and payables clerks, and finance business partners embedded in other
departments.

## Founder or chief executive (small companies only)

**Titles:** Founder, Co-Founder, CEO, Chief Executive Officer, Managing Director, Owner, President

**Description:** The top executive of a company small enough (under about 50 people) that they still
own the problem directly. A lower rung capped at one per company; in larger companies they delegate
and a function head is the right person. "Managing Director" is the UK and German form of chief
executive; "President" in the US may be the chief executive or a division head, so check the
company's size.

## Pitfalls seen in titles

- "Director" in agencies and banks is often a middle rank, not a function head.
- "Head of" in a 10-person team may be a one-person function.
- "Customer Success" is account management in business software, not support.
- "Operations" alone spans the warehouse to revenue operations: always qualify it.
- Recruiters for a function ("Talent Acquisition, Engineering") match the function's keywords.
