# Brief template

One markdown document: five lines on top, then six parts. Every item carries its source in square
brackets. Parts 1 to 4 and 6 come from the company's material, the person's words and the outreach
already run; a part with nothing behind it says "Not in the material." and the person is asked.
Part 5 is your own research and says so on every line.

## 0. In five lines

- **What we sell**: the offer in the company's own terms, what a buyer actually gets, not the
  category.
- **Who it is for**: who buys it, in one or two sentences a person would say, not a list of filters.
- **Pain removed**: what stops hurting for that buyer once they have bought.
- **Proof**: what a message may cite: named customers, specific figures, cases, awards,
  certifications. Only what a source names.
- **Never claim**: claims a message must not make (guarantees, figures, customers, capabilities no
  source supports, anything easy to overstate). One claim per line, written as the claim. Each is
  also a `rule` note.

## 1. About the company

- **Snapshot**: industry, size, geography, stage, key products.
- **Business model**: how it makes money, the average deal, the sales cycle, the channels.
- **Goals**: what the outbound is for, how many meetings a month, what a good meeting is.
- **Constraints**: compliance, tone, forbidden topics, what went badly before. Each hard
  constraint is also a `rule` note.

## 2. Product

- **What we sell**: the products and services, and what they are used for.
- **Pricing**: plans, prices (currency, period), the model. Copy never states a price the company
  has not approved for outreach.
- **Differentiators**: how it beats the alternatives and the status quo, as the company states it.
  Where part 5 shows an incumbent owns the same claim, say so.
- **Objections**: the frequent ones and the answers that work; those actually heard (part 6) first.
- **Cases**: the customer, the task, the result.

## 3. Who we sell to

- **Target companies**: industries, size, geography, technology; triggers such as hiring, funding,
  a launch. Feeds `segment-design`.
- **Target people**: who decides, who champions, who blocks, and the titles to search on. Feeds
  `persona-design`.
- **Qualification**: the facts a company must have to be worth writing to.
- **Who we do not touch**: segments, competitors, current customers, geographies. Each checkable
  item is also a do-not-contact entry or a `rule` note.

## 4. What we say

- **Pain and triggers**: what hurts in the segment and which events sharpen it.
- **Offer**: the value, the call to action, and something concrete and free the reader gets for a
  yes (a check, a list, a short review of their numbers). The first email asks for it.
- **Talking points**: by role.
- **Avoid**: topics, clichés, compliance.
- **Proof to use**: which case goes with which segment or objection.

Written after part 5, so the pain and the offer sit where the incumbents are not.

## 5. Market and competitors (research)

Every line carries `[research: <source>, <date>]`. Nothing here is a claim about the company.

- **The market**: size and direction, with the figure and who published it.
- **Incumbents**: the two or three buyers already pay for this job, what each sells and to whom.
- **What they claim**: each incumbent's lead claim, in its own words.
- **Ground we do not fight on**: what an incumbent owns that the company cannot claim or match.
- **How the company is classified**: by directories, review sites and professional networks, and
  which neighbours that puts it next to. A misleading neighbour is the reader's first objection;
  answer it in part 4.

## 6. Previous outreach

- **What ran**: campaigns, channels, dates, status.
- **Results by channel**: sends, replies and meetings per channel, as rates with their base ("email
  replies at 0.13% of sends, LinkedIn at 20% of the people who accepted").
- **What replies said**: objections and interest, quoted, with how many said each.
- **Meetings**: each one, with the channel, the role and the message it came from.
- **Audiences**: which lists were worked, where they came from, how many people.
- **Sending identity**: who signed, from which domains.
- **What it means for us**: the channel the evidence favours, the objection to answer first, the
  audiences to reuse or avoid.
- **Not obtained**: what was asked for and not received.

## Source markers

Concrete enough to find again: `[call 2026-09-12]`, `[deck p.7]`, `[site /pricing]`, `[email from
Jane 2026-09-20]`, `[research: a review site's category page, 2026-09-24]`, `[outreach: 2025
LinkedIn campaign export]`. When sources conflict on something acted on differently:

> Price per seat: $49 a month [site /pricing] against $39 a month on an annual plan [call
> 2026-09-12]; asked the person.
